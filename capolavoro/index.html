Meet Lorenzo

Hi, I am Lorenzo—though close friends often call me Lory, and here in Italy, most people simply know me by my surname, Rodoni. I am a student driven by technology, communication, and a constant desire to push my own boundaries.

I have a truly global outlook on learning. The "Capolavoro" Vision: A Living CV

In our educational journey, a Capolavoro project is not just a standard school assignment. It is a comprehensive, digital masterpiece designed to showcase what a student is truly capable of achieving. Think of this entire platform as a dynamic, interactive, "already-done CV"—a concrete proof of my work ethic, technical skills, and adaptability for future academic and professional paths.

Multi-Language Management

Holding a certified B1 in English and an A2 in French, I built this project to demonstrate that I can fluidly manage, structure, and switch between multiple languages simultaneously inside complex digital environments.

Independent Architecture

I engineered, structured, and wrote every single line of code for this website entirely by myself from scratch. No pre-made templates, no automated web builders—just clean code, planning, and execution.

Code & Problem Solving

Programming is more than just typing syntax; it is the ultimate exercise in logical problem-solving. Building this multi-page architecture forced me to debug routing, manage directory hierarchies, and resolve layout obstacles independently.

Deep-Dive Research

This project proves that I can take a sensitive, highly important social topic—Specific Learning Disorders (SLD/DSA)—conduct deep research, break down complex medical terms, and present them with empathy and clarity.

My Intentions: Why This Project Matters

"This project is a reflection of who I am and where I am heading. I chose to focus on Special Learning Disorders because inclusivity begins with awareness. Even though I do not personally have an SLD, I believe digital environments should be open, understandable, and accessible to everyone.

By coding this platform independently and configuring it across language barriers, I am proving to myself—and to my future—that I have the technical skills to build systems, the language skills to communicate globally, and the research skills to master any topic put in front of me. This is my work, my skill set, and my Capolavoro."
