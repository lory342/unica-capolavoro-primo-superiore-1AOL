The Confrontation: "He is just lazy"

This scene captures the exact moment the father’s "tough love" philosophy crashes into the reality of Ishaan’s neurobiology. Use the section below to explore the full dialogue.

Click to expand: The Japanese Box & The "Lazy" Argument

Father: "Look, Mr. Nikumbh, we’ve tried everything. But the boy is just... he’s just lazy. He has no desire to work. He’d rather play or draw than pick up a book. He’s avoiding his responsibilities, and he needs to learn that life is a race!"

Nikumbh: "Mr. Awasthi, do you really believe a child would choose to fail? Do you think he enjoys being humiliated by teachers and mocked by his friends every single day? It’s not a lack of 'desire.' It’s a lack of ability to decode the symbols you take for granted."

Father: "That’s just an excuse. If he tried harder, he could do it. It’s all about discipline."

[Nikumbh reaches for a box on the table with Japanese text.]

Nikumbh: "Alright. Since you believe it's just about 'trying harder,' please read the instructions on this box for me. Right now."

Father: "What is this? I can’t read this. It’s Japanese."

Nikumbh (Firmly): "No, don't be lazy, Mr. Awasthi! Use some discipline! Just read it! The letters are right there. Why are you being so stubborn? READ IT!"

Father (Frustrated): "Are you mad? I told you, I don't know the language! The characters make no sense to me, they are just lines and shapes!"

Nikumbh: "Exactly. And yet, you are shouting at an eight-year-old boy for the exact same thing. To Ishaan, English is Japanese. Math is Japanese. He isn't being 'lazy'—he is literally blind to the meaning of those shapes. Your 'discipline' is just making him feel like a failure for a condition he didn't choose."

[The Father stands in stunned silence as the realization finally hits him.]

What Happens in This Scene?

During this visit, Ishaan’s father defensively argues that his son is just lazy, insolent, and acting out. To break through this denial, the teacher tries a brilliant and direct approach. He pulls out an item with Chinese text written on the packaging and hands it to the father, firmly demanding that he read it aloud.

When the father grows angry and replies, "How can I? It's in Chinese!", the teacher explains that this exact feeling of helplessness, frustration, and confusion is what Ishaan experiences every single day. Letters and symbols on a page look completely foreign to a child with dyslexia, and shouting at them or punishing them will never change how their brain processes data.

The Lesson Behind the Clip

Nikumbh famously highlights that the child is not failing to read; rather, the standard education system is failing to teach him in a way he can understand. He points out that treating a neurobiological condition as a behavioral issue only strips away a child's dignity and self-esteem.

Key Takeaway: Inclusion begins with perspective. Just as a non-Chinese speaker is not "lazy" for failing to read Chinese characters, a student with Dyslexia is not "lazy" for struggling with traditional reading formats. Once the parents see the world through Ishaan's eyes, the path toward healing and proper educational support finally opens.
