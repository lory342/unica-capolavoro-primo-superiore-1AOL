What are SLDs?

Specific Learning Disorders (SLD), known in Italy as DSA (Disturbi Specifici dell'Apprendimento), are neurodevelopmental conditions that affect the brain's ability to process, store, or retrieve information. They are neurobiological variations, not intellectual deficits.

Students with SLD possess normal or above-average general intelligence (IQ). However, they experience significant challenges in specific academic skills such as reading, writing, and computing.

The Four Main Categories

SLDs are generally categorized into four distinct neurobiological conditions:

Dyslexia

Difficulty with accurate and fluent word recognition, decoding text, and spelling challenges.

Dysorthography

Difficulty in linguistic correctness during writing, involving phonological errors and grammar rule implementation.

Dysgraphia

A motor-graphical writing impairment that affects handwriting legibility, speed, and spatial coordination on paper.

Dyscalculia

Difficulty understanding number concepts, mathematical symbols, mental calculation, and numerical operations.

Compensatory Tools & Dispensatory Measures

To guarantee equal educational opportunities, Italian school legislation (Law 170/2010) safeguards students through tailored learning plans known as PDP (Piano Didattico Personalizzato).

Compensatory Tools (Strumenti Compensativi): Technologies or materials that substitute or ease the missing functional skill.

  • Text-to-speech software and audiobooks for reading challenges.
  • Concept maps, mind maps, and digital diagrams for information organization.
  • Calculators, formula sheets, and digital spellcheckers.

Dispensatory Measures (Misure Dispensative): Strategies that allow the student to avoid performing specific burdensome tasks without shifting educational targets.

  • Allowing extra time during written tests (usually 30% more time).
  • Prioritizing oral examinations over extensive written papers.
  • Exemption from reading long texts aloud in front of the classroom.
Crucial Takeaway: These strategies do not create unfair advantages; instead, they level the playing field, shifting the focus to actual knowledge rather than neurobiological obstacles.
